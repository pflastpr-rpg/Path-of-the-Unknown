Introduction

What Is IronTithe?

You begin this game as a blank sheet of parchment — a hollow vessel of bone and muscle. There are no classes to guide you; your choices are the only ink available. Every time you sharpen a skill, forge a weapon, or make a desperate bargain to survive an encounter, you are adding permanent marks to your ledger. You can endure the first mark. You can manage the second. But the moment you are IronTithe, you have hit the absolute ceiling of physics, magic, and biology. The game is an accounting of how close you are to that third mark — and what you are willing to look like before you break.

You need: this document, one ten-sided die (1d10) per player, and people willing to find out what their character is made of.

The Unified Rule of Three

The number three is the structural limit of this world. It operates on three simultaneous scales:

Column I — Body Column II — Mind Column III — Soul
The Power of Three
Every 3 Skill Points in a category forces the parent characteristic up by +1. The body is sculpted by what it survives.		 The Condition Matrix
A 3rd simultaneous condition triggers Overwhelmed — the mind buckles under what the body still carries. There is a ceiling to what can be endured at once.		 The Reaching Price
Reach past the power you have earned, in a blessing, a spell, a desperate working, and the Weave takes its due from your own essence. To grasp beyond your soul’s measure is always paid for.
Three Questions Every Veteran Will Ask

What is marked upon my character sheet? An active, evolving ledger of systemic debt. Every row begins empty. You force the world to carve a path into it.

Who is doing the marking? The world marks you from every side — faith and its institutions press for your obedience, the contested borderlands take their toll in blood, and stranger things still reach for you from the dark beyond the maps. These are only some of the hands that hold the pen. To survive is to choose, again and again, whose ink you are willing to wear.

What happens when the third mark lands? You reach the Threshold — the point where the world has taken its full measure of you, and the cost of all you have survived comes due at once. What waits there is the gravest peril a character can face, and the complete rules tell how it resolves. Much of the game is the quiet arithmetic of how near that third mark you are willing to walk — and what you are prepared to spend before you get there.

The World in One Paragraph
The world has already survived one invasion—though survival is a generous term for a delayed execution. The Aakzad, cold harvesters from the dark between stars, were never defeated, only entombed. Now, something has stolen the largest fragment of their shattered artefact, and the long awakening has begun. Into this fraying reality step four peoples, seven disciplines of power, and an unknown number of mortals who have decided their purpose is worth the iron toll it exacts. They walk a world bound by the Church of the Radiant Compact, which enforces theological unity across the Human kingdoms while meticulously guarding the deliberate, bleeding gaps in its own historical record. In the margins of this human tide, the elder races wait: the Dwarves of Kel Thurum hold the mountains, the silver city of Thalasien holds its cold silence, and the Halflings hold ancient knowledge that no outsider will ever be permitted to learn.
Chapter I

The Core Mechanic

IronTithe uses a single die: the 1d10. Almost every action that matters is resolved the same way: roll 1d10, add relevant bonuses, meet or beat a Target Number (TN). If you do, you succeed. If you don't, consequences follow.

The Core Roll
1d10 + your relevant bonuses, to meet or beat a Target Number

Roll one ten-sided die and add what you have earned: the points you have trained into the relevant skill, sharpened by who your character is. If the total meets or beats the TN set by the GM, or by the rules, you succeed. (The complete rules spell out exactly which bonuses apply to each kind of action; this Quickstart keeps it simple.)

Advantage: Roll 2d10 and take the higher result. Granted by skills, positioning, or circumstances in your favour.

Disadvantage: Roll 2d10 and take the lower result. Imposed by injury, environment, or being outmatched.

The Decisive Strike (Natural 10)
When a To-Hit roll lands and the unmodified d10 shows a natural 10, the strike bites deep: the attacker tears an extra die of wounds from the enemy. In the hands of the truly deadly, one perfect strike can open into another, and another still. The full rules give the exact sequence; for now, know that a natural 10 is where butchery begins.
The Arcane Backlash (Natural 1)
An unmodified 1 is an absolute failure, whatever you were attempting. On an attack, a natural 1 always misses; no skill or bonus can save it. On a spell, it is worse: the weave is volatile, the spirits fickle, the gods demanding of perfection. A natural 1 To-Cast triggers a catastrophic backfire from the Miscast/Wrath table. In this Quickstart, on such a Backlash, the spell fails and the caster loses 1d10 wounds from the feedback. Luck can blunt this fate, though never erase it: a point of Luck spent on a natural 1 cannot turn ruin into success, but it spares you the backlash. The spell still fails and the Tithe is still paid; you simply do not bleed for the weave tearing loose.

The contest rule. When two wills clash, both roll 1d10 + skill and the higher total wins (ties favour the defender). If you have no training in the skill, you fall back on raw nature — Disadvantage on the d10 + the governing characteristic. Two skills, Inspire and Performance, add their characteristic even when trained, as uncontested projection.

Resist Bonus: creatures defend by a standing bar: a Characteristic TN plus a Resist bonus that scales with their Tier, so a tougher foe is genuinely harder to sway, frighten, or dominate.

Chapter II

Action Points & Combat Flow

Time on the battlefield is measured in heartbeats and drawn steel. Every combatant is granted a base of 1 Action Point (AP) per exchange. This pool is expanded through the Quickness skill or deliberate investment of Experience.

AP Carry-Over: For every 3 points of base AP a character has, they may carry 1 unused AP into their next turn, declared at the end of that turn. A character with 3 base AP can carry 1; with 6 they can carry 2. Carried AP must be spent on the very next turn or it fades to nothing; it cannot be hoarded.
Damage Mitigation
When you take a hit, you don't always take the full wounds. Damage Mitigation (DM) reduces incoming wounds by a flat amount, drawn from armour, skills, or abilities. If DM equals or exceeds wounds dealt, you take 0 wounds from that hit. DM does not apply to falling, environmental hazards, or poison damage unless a skill specifically states otherwise.
The Tithe
Every wound is a collection. When blood is spilled — theirs or yours — the world takes its due; the IronTithe is always counting. Yet the ledger runs both ways. Spill enough, prevail, and the world that bled you may find itself in your debt — repaying you not in mended flesh, for healing is no part of this bargain, but in raw power drawn from the spending. The full reckoning of the Tithe forms the beating heart of the complete rules. For now, know only this: nothing bleeds for free.

How to Read Mira's Stats

Chapter IV

The Devotion Discipline

Mira carries 3 points in Devotion — enough to call down the lesser blessings reliably. To cast, she rolls 1d10 + her To-Cast bonus against the blessing's TN; with steady faith the holy light answers, and only the more demanding miracles sit beyond her easy reach. In a desperate hour she may still reach for a blessing greater than her devotion has earned — but the gods exact a higher price from those they judge unready, and such overreaching can wound the one who dares it. The full rules give the measure of that gamble.

Mira can hold 3 blessings in memory — equal to her Devotion skill points. She must choose which to prepare before play begins. Below are her available blessings for this quickstart.

Chapter V

The First Encounter

Use this encounter to learn the system. Three Aakzad Warriors have located Mira in the ruins of a village they emptied three days ago. They were not searching for her. They simply noted her presence as a variable.

Aakzad Warrior
Construct · Biomechanical · ×3
Wounds 18
Defence 11
WM 2
AP 2
Attack: Bone-blade strike — 1d10 + 3 to hit, deals 1d10 + 2 wounds.
Frequency Pulse (1/turn): Free action. Any other Warrior within 30ft gains +1 to their next To-Hit roll — they are communicating.
Immunity: Fear, Charm, Poison. They do not feel these things.
Weakness: Take +2 wounds from Devotion blessings and Shaman spirit attacks.
Running the Encounter — GM Notes
Round 1: The Warriors advance in formation. They do not rush. One moves into melee range, the second prepares to strike (holds 1 AP as reaction), the third uses Frequency Pulse to link them. Mira gets the first action — she bought initiative with her Quickness investment.

Escalation: If Mira uses Turn Undead, remind the player it affects Undead — Aakzad Warriors are Constructs, not Undead. This matters. It is not a mistake in the rules. It is information about what she is facing.

Victory Condition: Reduce all three to 0 wounds, or escape the engagement. To flee the fray, roll to Disengage (1d10 + Agility) — a low roll lets a Warrior in melee range land a parting strike against your retreating back.
Step-by-Step: Mira's First Turn
1. Declare actions — Mira has 2 AP. She declares: Attack (1 AP) + Cast Lesser Heal on herself if she took damage in the surprise round (1 AP).

2. To-Hit roll — 1d10 + 1 (To-Hit) = result; her Edged proficiency means she rolls without penalty, and the +1 is her veteran's edge. Enemy Defence is 11, so a 10 or better lands the blow.

3. Damage roll — Longsword: 1d10 + Strength Conditioning bonus (+1) = wounds dealt. Enemy DM of 2 reduces this. Remainder applied to their 18 wounds. (Physique modifier is 0 at score 5 — the +1 comes from Strength Conditioning, not the characteristic.)

4. Natural 10? The strike is decisive — roll an extra 1d10 for bonus wounds. No bonuses apply to this roll.

5. Enemy response — one Warrior attacks back: 1d10 + 3 vs Mira's Defence 12. If they hit, roll 1d10 + 2 wounds. Mira's DM 3 reduces this.
The Threshold of Collapse: If Mira acquires three different conditions simultaneously (Bleeding + Shaken + Stunned, for example), she is Overwhelmed — she cannot take any Reaction Actions until one condition is cleared. The Warriors know this. They apply conditions deliberately.
