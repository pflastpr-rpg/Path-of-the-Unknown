Khugron — The Gateway to the Deep Session 03
Resolved

The air inside Khugron was a choking fog of rancid grease and the metal tang of raw meat. The party stood in the aftermath of their own ambush, the garrison's mess hall gone quiet around the ruined dead — an Ogre that had served as the butcher, a handful of Hobgoblin guards, and a Chaos Dwarf bearing the industrial hallmarks of Birn Uluhm. The long tables were scarred from years of cleaving, and beneath the grime someone had scratched a crude map into the wood: a deep shaft, marked with the crest of Birn Uluhm. Beside it sat a detail that asked an uglier question — the dented pewter bowls were stamped with the house marks of SunsReach. Somewhere in that distant port, a merchant was provisioning this place for coin.

The quiet broke before they could hold it. A lone Hobgoblin, seeing what had been done to the hall, bolted from the shadows and ran to wake the mountain. The party split on instinct — Leon planting himself in the barracks archway while Dresin tore off down the corridor after the runner.

At the archway, javelins came out of the dark and sparked uselessly off Leon's armour. Bannon answered from behind him: his first arrow went wide, but the second found the gap in a Hobgoblin's iron and dropped it where it stood, breaking the shield wall. Leorlian spoke a single word and a wave of sleep rolled over the formation, leaving the last Hobgoblin slumped on the stone and the armoured Chaos Dwarf alone and exposed. The Lava-Forged thing stepped over the sleeping goblin and came on hard, and Fight n Killin met it — his flail crashing past the iron shield, breaking the warrior's guard, then coming around in two more arcs that left nothing of the sleeping goblin to wake.

Down the corridor, Dresin's pursuit ended at a pair of double doors and the Hobgoblins guarding them. He took a javelin for it, then flung up a wall of thorned vines across the threshold to keep whatever waited inside from spilling out. One Hobgoblin tore free; the vines took the other. Bleeding, Dresin drew on the deep stone beneath him and knitted his own wounds closed, then loosed a blinding light that punched through the survivor's guard and left it groping at nothing.

Then the room at the end of the hall emptied into them. A Hobgoblin Champion — a scarred brute with a serrated greatsword — pushed through the vines with two more of its kind at its back, and for a moment the fight turned against the party. Leorlian was caught in the open and cut down twice, dropped to the stone and brought back each time by Dresin's healing, the old druidic mending reaching across the press just fast enough to keep the Wizard's heart going. Bannon emptied his quiver into the rear of the press to clear it, and Fight n Killin forced his way to the front of the chokepoint to crack the Champion's bones with his flail. When the corridor threatened to break open entirely, Dresin reached down into the mountain and called up an Earth Elemental, dropping it into the passage as a living wall of stone and soil between the party and everything still trying to reach them.

The Champion's quarters gave up their secrets grudgingly, and meanly. A throne built from the scavenged armour of the dead concealed a ring of hidden vents, and it scalded both Fight n Killin and Dresin with geysers of superheated steam before Dresin found the lever to kill it; behind the cooled plates, a hidden compartment held five hundred gold. More telling than the coin was a wax-sealed scroll tube bearing the command insignia of Kel Thurum — the uncorrupted dwarven capital. Inside was a field order: an eight-man scouting party sent to look at Khugron's newly carved maw, with explicit instructions not to press deep. The tally marks gouged into the walls told the rest. They had pressed deep. Among the spoils, Fight n Killin found and drained two draughts of fortitude, a locked chest gave up rations and silver and a letter in Hobgoblin script none of them could read, and buried in an Ogre-sized cutting board was a great axe that hummed with old enchantment.

The stair beyond the hall fell away into a colossal vertical shaft, and the thing working the mountain's elevator confirmed how far the rot had gone: a massive Earth Elemental, bound in glowing brass runes and chained to the winch, made to haul the platform up and down against its will. The party had little time to weigh it — Dresin threw open the wrong door and found three Chaos Dwarves at their weapons, one swinging a blunderbuss toward the gap. As a hidden Overseer bellowed the alarm, Dresin tore a wall of mud and earth out of the floor and brought it down on the advancing dwarves, crushing them back and sealing the corridor behind a barrier they would be a long while digging through. Then the party rode the platform down, hundreds of feet into rising heat, the cold subterranean air giving way to a sweltering orange glow.

The lower hall held twin armouries of newly forged weapons, clean and gleaming and untouched by the rust of the garrison above. But the makers' marks came from smiths scattered across the continent, and a shipment manifest by the door carried logistics codes that matched Kel Thurum itself — proof of a supply chain reaching into, or out of, the dwarven capital. Deeper among the racks lay finer things: dwarven plate, great hammers, another enchanted axe, heavy shields, all of it waiting. Further on, the corridor narrowed to a fortified chokepoint, and at its end stood an Ogre-sized mechanised construct — a Dreadnought, motionless, guarding whatever lay in the vault behind it. The party looked at it, weighed it against the dwarven voices still echoing from the locked rooms behind them, and chose not to wake it.

The eastern tunnel offered no better welcome. It opened onto a magma-lit hellscape of veined fire and soot-black stone, the roar of liquid rock broken only by the crack of whips and the cries of labourers far below. They had found the heart of the operation at last — the Chaos Forge of Birn Uluhm, a full-scale slaving works carved into the mountain's gut. Battered, burned, and running short on everything, the party held at the threshold rather than throw themselves into it. The Forge, and the choice of what to do about it, would keep until they were ready.

The Sunken Archive Session 02
Resolved

The party infiltrated the Sunken Archive — a submerged complex of pentagon-shaped chambers sealed by a Lizardman Shamanic lock.


Key Events:
  • The Trance: Dresin heard Spirit-Echo broadcasts escalating through progressive Resist tests (7, 8, then 12 at the door). Enthralled, he spoke the anti-magical counter-lock and the Aakzad resonant language. The gateway opened.
  • The Emergence: Ten sets of green glowing eyes. Four Aakzad stepped forward defensively, speaking: "For your sacrifice we shall now let you take this moment to leave these chambers for we cannot further protect you from the others once they arrive."
  • The Split: Six Aakzad divided into two groups of three, moving east and west into the deeper complex with evident purpose.
  • The Confrontation: 20 Lizardmen, 40 Skinks, 1 Skink High Priest surrounding the entrance. Pirate ship sailing off. One pirate dead, filled with darts. A Lizardman bone-ship offshore.
  • The High Priest: "Your ignorance and weak will has unleashed a great evil upon these lands." Delivered the scroll. Threatened death if the party refuses the mission.
