The Philosophy

No Class. No Destiny.
Only What You Earn.

Scars and Purpose is a classless, skill-based dark fantasy tabletop RPG. There are no pre-built archetypes. No wizard class that tells you what you are, no fighter template that decides how you fight. You build the hero you want from the ground up — investing Experience Points in the skills, characteristics, and disciplines that define your play style.

You are not born to a role. You are hammered into one. The world is hard, consequence is real, and nothing is given. Every scar on your character sheet was earned at a price your character chose to pay.

01
Classless
Six characteristics. Thirty-seven primary skills. Six magic disciplines. Any combination is possible. A Halfling can wade through slaughter. A Dwarf can master the arcane. The world does not care what you were born to be.
02
Skill-Based
Every point matters. Skills have tiered benefits at 3, 6, and 9 points — meaningful upgrades that reward commitment. Investing deep in two or three areas produces a dramatically more effective hero than spreading thin across everything.
03
Consequential
Conditions stack. Wounds accumulate. The Threshold of Collapse is real — three concurrent conditions and your body begins to shut down. The battlefield does not pause for hesitation.
04
Purpose-Driven
Before play begins, every character answers three questions: What do they want? What do they fear? What do they carry from their past? Mechanics decide outcomes. The reasons you care about those outcomes are yours to write.

The world of Scars and Purpose is profoundly indifferent to the legacy of your birth. It does not care what you were born to be; it measures you only by the actions you take and the scars you leave behind.

— Core Rulebook, Chapter I
The Setting

A World That Has Already Seen Too Much

The world of Scars and Purpose is not young. It bears the geological weight of civilisations that rose, overreached, and were buried by what they disturbed. The Aakzad — a biomechanical intelligence from the dark between stars — invaded during the First Age and were not destroyed, only entombed. Their psychic pull never ceased. Something has recently stolen the largest fragment of their shattered artefact. The mass awakening has already begun.

Into this world walk four races, six magical traditions, and an unknown number of people who have decided that their purpose is worth the price.

⛰
Kel Thurum
The great Dwarven mountain kingdom. Every citizen is militia. Every miner has spilled blood. A philosophy carved in stone: the city outlasts the individual.
🏛
Thalasien
The silver city of the Elves. Cold beauty, calculated architecture, and a Golden Age that ended in something the scholars describe only with academic citations.
⛪
The Radiant Compact
The closest thing humanity has to a universal authority. Its dogma travels faster than any army. The gaps in its own historical record are methodically maintained.
☠
The Aakzad
Ancient biomechanical intelligence. They do not seek conquest — they harvest the frequency of life itself. Where they walk, the living are not killed. They are emptied.
🌿
The Deep Earth
The Titan-Blood veins that corrupted the Birn clan have never been fully mapped. The question of whether they remain active is one Kel Thurum officially considers closed.
🌑
The Unknown
The Halflings possess oral histories of the world before the other races arrived. What the world was then is knowledge they share with no outsider. Their silence yields no answers.
🩸
The Pale Weald
The Blight Elves do not hate those they raid. Hatred is an expenditure. They regard the north with the patient attention of a harvester regarding a field. The field's opinion of the harvest is not a variable.
🔥
The Ashlands
The Pyreborn nomads carry Titan-Blood in their ancestry — touched by the same deep-earth corruption that took the Birn clan. They name Aethon the World-Breaker as a cousin. They do not consider this a comforting relationship.
⚠ The Opening Hook
The fragment stolen from SunsReach is the largest of three pieces of the Death Priest's shattered artefact. The other two remain unaccounted for. Whether the groundskeeper Amako "Kazamas" Pikari acted alone — or was drawn to the tomb by the Death Priest's psychic reach — is a question no one has yet asked in the right room. The Aakzad awakening has already begun. The world does not yet know it.

The World's Other Peoples

The World Does Not Belong Only to Those Who Claim It

Beyond the four playable races, the world is populated by peoples who have their own histories, their own grievances, and their own answers to the question of survival. They are not background dressing. They are forces.

🌑
Blight Elves
Cousins of Thalasien, shaped by centuries in the Pale Weald's corrupted shadow. Raiders by necessity, methodical by nature. They do not hate what they take. Hatred is an expenditure they cannot afford.
🔥
Pyreborn
Ashlands nomads with Titan-Blood ancestry. Fire-touched, war-hardened, carrying oral traditions that predate the mountain kingdoms. They name the World-Breaker as kin. This does not make them comfortable to know.
🐗
Beastmen
Warband-organised, theologically certain, and led by Horned Oracles who find power in the rotting of things. They consider decay more fundamental than destruction. They are not wrong.
⚔
The Orcish Host
To lead requires not merely strength but violent relentlessness applied so consistently no challenger can find the gap. The Chieftains have been finding gaps in every other army's line for three centuries.
👁
Daemon Lords
They do not arrive with fire and spectacle. They arrive with dark majesty and the patience of something that already knows the shape of the room. They are almost always already seated when you notice them.
🦴
The Gnolls
They do not hold territory the way settled peoples hold territory — they hold the idea of it. Armies that have cleared Gnoll lands have cleared the Gnolls that were there. Then dealt with the Gnolls that came back.

Collectively, they are a sprawling, unpredictable equation that the rest of the world is still bleeding to solve.

— On Humanity, Core Rulebook Chapter I
The Six Disciplines

Magic Is Not Safe. It Was Never Meant to Be.

Six magical traditions exist in this world. Each draws from a different source, answers to different laws, and carries different consequences. A Natural 1 on any casting roll is an Arcane Backlash — the weave is volatile, the spirits are fickle, the gods demand perfection. An unmitigated failure triggers immediate consequences from the Miscast table.

Not all disciplines coexist peacefully. Wizardry and Runecraft are philosophically and practically opposed. Combining them is not merely forbidden — it is an incoherence the universe refuses to permit.
