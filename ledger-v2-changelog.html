The Ledger v2
What's New

A quick guide to what changed, so you can decide whether to adopt it. The short version: v2 keeps everything you know and adds one big upgrade to how skills work, plus a handful of rules clarifications. Your existing characters still make sense — nothing about your concept breaks.

⚔ The Big Change: How Skills Work

v1 — Old Each of your six characteristics had six fixed skills. Everyone's Agility was always the same six — Athletics, Evasion, Quickness, Stealth, Thieving, Tumble. That was that.
v2 — New Each characteristic now has 3 locked Core skills + 3 Swap slots you choose from a larger pool. You build the skill list that fits your character.

The 3 Core skills are always present — the fundamentals (Agility's core is Athletics, Evasion, Quickness). The 3 Swap slots let you pick from a wider pool to fit your concept. Agility's pool now includes Acrobatics, Disguise, Escapology, and Sleight of Hand alongside the old options.

Why it's better: two Agility characters no longer have to be mechanically identical. A cat-burglar takes Sleight of Hand and Escapology; an acrobat-duelist takes Acrobatics and Tumble. You build to your concept instead of inheriting a fixed six.

The catch (by design): a swap slot commits once you invest a point in it. To change a committed slot you use Reformation — the GM lets you refund and rebuild, recovering 1 XP for every 3 spent there. Choices matter, but they aren't permanent prison sentences.

New skills now reachable in the pools:

Acrobatics · Disguise · Escapology · Sleight of Hand · Danger Sense · Lip Reading · Swift Read · Deception · Intimidation · Performance · Seduction · Alchemy · Engineering · Medicine · Navigation · Brawling · Climbing · Endurance · Swimming · Meditation · Oathkeeper · Pain Tolerance · Willpower Reserve — and the Lost Seventh Discipline, Śabda-Axiom, is now selectable in the Willpower pool.

᛭ Rules Updates

Clarifications and balance passes the whole system adopted — reflected in v2, and worth knowing about regardless of which ledger you use.

Devotion replaces "Faith" The Charisma discipline is now called Devotion everywhere — same mechanic, clearer name.
Devotion armour rule simplified Casting Devotion in armour used to mean halving a fluctuating penalty. Now it's clean: no penalty in light armour (TN 3 or lower — up to banded/splint), a flat −2 in heavy armour (TN 4+). Easier to remember, friendlier to armoured clerics.
The Rule of Three is clearer With 3 or more total conditions (of any kind — three Bleeds counts, so does one Bleed + two Poison), you're Overwhelmed: no Reaction actions. The tracker now stays dormant until you actually hit 3, then lights up — no more guessing whether you've crossed the line.
Disengage fixed Disengaging now rolls 1d10 + Agility Bonus (the old version referenced a skill that no longer exists).
Dwarf — Grudge-Forged now ramps Instead of a flat +3 wounds the instant something draws your blood, the grudge deepens: +1 wound the first round, rising +1 each round it endures, to a maximum of +3. Rewards the long, bitter fight — which is the whole point of a grudge.
Elf — Precise Strike cost adjusted Precise Strike now costs Elves 45 XP (was 40), in line with Humans and Dwarves. Elves keep their signature discounts on Wizardry, Athletics, Stealth, and the agility skills — this just removes a double-dip, since the Elf's permanent +1 Awareness already boosts every strike.

✓ What Did NOT Change

  • Your characteristics, wounds, and the core dice system work exactly as before.
  • Every skill you already use still exists — Core skills cover the fundamentals, and anything else moved into a pool you can still pick.
  • Individual skill XP costs are unchanged (except the one Elf Precise Strike adjustment above).
  • Disciplines, magic, combat math, and advancement all behave the same.

Should You Switch?

Adopt v2 if you want more distinct characters — the swap system is the whole reason it exists. Two rogues, two clerics, two rangers can finally feel genuinely different in their skills.

Stick with v1 if your table prefers the simplicity of a fixed list and would rather not think about swap slots or Reformation.

Either way, the rules updates above are worth adopting regardless — they're clarifications and fixes, not version-locked features. Try v2 with one character, see how the build feels, and decide from there.
