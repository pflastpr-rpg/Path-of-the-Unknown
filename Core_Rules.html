The Foundation

The Playable Races

Four peoples walk the unforgiving earth of this world. Your bloodline dictates the architecture of your bones, the swiftness of your stride, and the volume of punishment your flesh can endure before breaking. Yet pedigree is not destiny. A Halfling may wade through slaughter with a veteran's grim efficiency, just as a Dwarf may dissect and master the volatile weave of the arcane. The world of Blood and Purpose is profoundly indifferent to the legacy of your birth. It does not care what you were born to be; it measures you only by the actions you take and the scars you leave behind.

Dwarf
HEIGHT 3.5–5 ft WEIGHT 120–250 lbs MOVE 12 DISTANCE 60 ft

Of all the peoples who walk the surface world, none wear their history as visibly as the Dwarves. It is etched into their posture — a rigid solidity, shoulders squared against a burden unlifted for a millennium. It is carved into their faces, which do not soften with age like those of Men, but weather into harsh, geological topography. They are forged for the deep: dense, corded with muscle born not of mere training, but of ancestral labour within suffocating stone corridors where there is no room to swing, only to drive forward. Their skin bears the permanent kiss of the forge — ruddy copper, ashen grey, or the burnished obsidian of those who have stood too close to the fire. Their hair, when not singed away by the heat, is bound in heavy iron rings, or shorn entirely to make canvas for the ink of clan-runes. They do not wither as shorter-lived peoples do. A Dwarf of a century remains in their prime. Yet, as they pass their second century, a profound stillness settles behind their eyes. The young of other races mistake this for distance; in truth, it is the quiet, crushing gravity of having outlived the very things they built.

Their masterwork is Kel Thurum. Some call it a city, others a kingdom; in truth, it is a philosophy carved in stone. The mountain halls descend in tiers that consumed four generations to hollow, illuminated by geothermal veins the Dwarves harnessed before mankind had learned to name itself. Its civic tradition rests on a single, absolute premise: the city outlasts the individual, and the individual serves the city. Every citizen of Kel Thurum is militia. Every miner has spilled blood. The distinction between soldier and craftsman is a surface-world luxury. A Dwarf of Kel Thurum is both, or they are nothing.

Yet a festering wound lies beneath this pride. The Birn clan is a scar on Dwarven memory. They were once of Kel Thurum — ambitious, gifted, the kind of deep-delvers who pressed into the dark when others declared a seam barren. What they unearthed in their deepest excavations remains a matter of terrified debate, but the consequences are undeniable. Exposure to what the oldest texts name Titan-Blood warped them, slowly and then absolutely, into an abomination the mountain could no longer abide. Their exile was struck from the annals without ceremony. What they became is whispered only in nightmares. A Dwarf who leaves the mountain carries both the unyielding pride of Kel Thurum and its darkest shadow: the knowledge that the deep earth harbours ancient things entirely indifferent to the ambitions of mortals.

[Lore Hook] The vein of Titan-Blood that corrupted the Birn has never been fully mapped. Orthodox scholars claim the seam was permanently sealed upon the clan's exile. The question of whether it remains — and whether it still pulses — is a matter the ruling council has officially closed, yet unofficially bleeds the treasury to answer.

Elf
HEIGHT 5–6.5 ft WEIGHT 90–170 lbs MOVE 18 DISTANCE 90 ft

The Elves do not claim to be the firstborn of the world. Certainty is a luxury afforded only to the short-lived, who will perish long before they can be corrected. The Elves have walked the earth long enough to carry grief as a second tongue — fluent, unsurprised, and worn with the cold composure of a people who decided, centuries ago, to stop bleeding where others could see.

They are tall and spare, carved with a ruthless, angular economy. They move with the unhurried precision of those who have simply run out of reasons to rush. Their eyes bear colours that do not occur in the natural world — pale amber, necrotic violet, reflective silver — and possess a gaze defined not by distance, but by an abyssal depth. By the time an Elf reaches their fifth century, they have watched their monuments lose their purpose, and everyone they loved become ash and legend.

The city of Thalasien is their masterwork and their burden. It is a settlement of cold, silver beauty, its architecture as calculated as Elven speech — nothing placed carelessly, nothing wasted, every arch and spire carrying the weight of a decision made once and never revisited. Their relationship with the arcane is not spiritual, but clinical. They dissect the weave of magic as a mortician dissects a corpse, operating on the belief that absolute understanding is the only defence against absolute subjugation. This cold arithmetic has made them the most formidable arcane scholars alive. It has also left them profoundly isolated. Scholarship at that depth is a solitary burial, and five centuries of it leaves scars that cannot be translated to fleeting, younger races. The world calls this arrogance. The Elves call it accuracy.

[Lore Hook] The Elves speak of a Golden Age with the sterile neutrality of those who were present for its slaughter. What precisely ended it — what cracked the original foundations of Thalasien — is a question their scholars will answer with academic citations, but never with candour. The wound is old enough to have become ceremonial. That does not mean it has healed.

Halfling
HEIGHT 3–4 ft WEIGHT 60–135 lbs MOVE 12 DISTANCE 60 ft

The Halflings are the most consistently underestimated people in the known world, a deception they have spent millennia meticulously cultivating. Barely four feet at their apex, they bear the wide, open faces of a people who learned early that perceived harmlessness is an impenetrable armour. Their feet are broad and heavily calloused, preferring the bare earth; soles thick as cured hide, yet sensitive enough to read the ground's tremors as a scholar reads text. Their hands are blunt and dexterous, carrying the inherited knowledge of fingers that know the exact threshold between living soil and dead rot.

Do not mistake their warmth for fragility. They are the first to laugh and the last to break, and the space between those two states is a lethal trap. They are patient in the manner of the deep earth: absolute, silent, and possessing a long, unforgiving memory.

Their connection to the living world is not a faith, nor a studied discipline. It is an intrinsic, biological anchor. A Halfling who sleeps on bare dirt wakes knowing the weather three days hence. One who presses a palm to ancient bark reads the tree's violent history in vibrations through the bone. They do not worship nature; they are continuous with it. They settle near ancient groves, river confluences, and hillsides where the earth remembers old bloodshed. What other races dismiss as simple agriculture, the Halflings execute as a deliberate, symbiotic tending.

[Lore Hook] Halfling oral tradition preserves accounts of the world before the arrival of the other races — before the Dwarven mountain-holds, before the silver spires of Thalasien, before mankind had learned to name its cities. What the world was then, and what the Halflings were within it, is a history they share with no outsider. Whether this silence is born of caution, grief, or something far worse is a question that yields no answers, only a sudden, chilling stillness.

Human
HEIGHT 5–7 ft WEIGHT 100–300 lbs MOVE 15 DISTANCE 75 ft

Humans are the most populous race in the known world, and from this sheer volume they have forged a sprawling, contradictory mass of civilisations — each fanatically convinced it is the apex of culture. They are physically volatile: compact or towering, pale or dark, shaped by ten thousand years of restless, violent migration across every forgiving and unforgiving terrain. They live barely a century. They build as though they will live forever.

This fleeting mortality is their most dangerous weapon. A Dwarf builds for the mountain. An Elf builds for posterity. A Human builds to outrun the grave. This panic breeds a people who are simultaneously the world's greatest architects and its most reliable source of catastrophic overreach. Their kingdoms are vast and fractious, bound by trade routes that double as battlelines. They are unified primarily by the Church of the Radiant Compact — a faith whose dogma travels faster than any army, and whose institutional relationship with the truth is deliberately unrecorded.

Humans do not specialise; they consume. Every culture they touch is assimilated, violently or quietly, then renamed and claimed as their own invention. The elder races find this relentless tide exhausting, yet none have managed to halt it. Individually, a Human is fragile beside an Elf's precision or a Dwarf's endurance. Collectively, they are a sprawling, unpredictable equation that the rest of the world is still bleeding to solve.

[Lore Hook] The Church of the Radiant Compact is the closest entity humanity has to a universal authority. It also possesses the most glaring voids in its own historical record — massacres, pacts, and cataclysms meticulously noted in the margins of Elven and Dwarven histories, yet cleanly excised from the Church's vaults. Whether this erasure is born of incompetence or ruthless policy is a question the inquisitors ensure is never asked aloud.

Core Characteristics

Every character in Blood and Purpose is defined by six primary characteristics. These are not just numbers — they represent the fundamental nature of who your character is and how they move through a dangerous world. Each characteristic governs a cluster of skills and provides passive bonuses to rolls, wounds caused, and tests.

Characteristics start at a base value determined by your race and the XP you invest at character creation. They grow slowly through play — raising a characteristic is expensive and deliberate, not something that happens by accident.

Your Agility modifier applies to Defense and to movement-based tests. Stamina also lives here: the body's capacity to endure is as much about reading your own limits as pushing past them. Acute Senses, Druidism, Focus, Precise Strike, Riposte, Stamina

Character Creation

Blood and Purpose uses a classless, skill-based system. There are no pre-built archetypes to slot into — you build the hero you want from the ground up, spending XP on the skills and characteristics that define your play style. The steps below walk you through the process from blank page to ready-to-play character.

1 Choose Your Race

Select one of the four playable races: Dwarf, Elf, Halfling, or Human. Your race sets your base physical statistics — height, weight, movement speed, and your starting wound total. It does not restrict what skills or disciplines you can pursue. A Halfling can be a hardened warrior. A Dwarf can master Wizardry. Your race is your origin, not your ceiling.

2 Allocate 300 XP

You begin with a pool of 300 Experience Points to spend on skills and characteristics. This is your starting budget — enough to build a competent hero but not a master of everything. Every choice matters. Skills are purchased from the Master Skill List; each skill has an XP cost per point and a maximum rank. Characteristics cost more to raise than skills and provide passive bonuses across every relevant roll.

A few principles to guide your spending:

  • Pick a focus. 300 XP spread thin across everything produces a hero who is mediocre at everything. A hero who specialises in two or three areas will be dramatically more effective early.
  • Don't forget survival. Skills like Fortitude (extra wounds), Evasion (better defense), and Iron Will (resist magic) are easy to overlook and painful to regret.
  • Magic is an investment. Arcane and divine disciplines require their associated characteristic to be meaningful. A Wizard who has not invested in Intellect will find their spells underwhelming.
  • Action Points matter. Investing in Quickness early gives you more actions per turn — one of the most impactful advantages in combat.

3 Calculate Derived Traits

Once your skills and characteristics are set, calculate the following:

  • Total Wounds: Start with your racial base wounds. Add your Physique characteristic modifier. Add any bonus wounds from skills such as Fortitude, Rugged, or Hard as Nails. This is how much punishment you can absorb before going down.
  • Base Defense: Your Defense determines how hard you are to hit. It starts at a base value modified by your Agility characteristic and any armor worn. Higher Agility means harder to strike — invest in it if you plan to stay light.
  • Wound Mitigation: Some armor types reduce wounds received by a flat amount. This is separate from Defense — Defense determines whether you are hit; Wound Mitigation reduces the damage when you are.
  • Action Points: Base 1 AP per turn, increased by the Quickness skill and by direct XP purchase. Calculate your total and note your carry-over potential using the Power of 3 rule.
  • Resist Bonus: Derived from Willpower. This is added to tests made to resist magical effects, fear, and conditions targeting the mind.

4 Equip Your Hero

You begin play with a starting gold allocation determined by the GM (typically 50–100 gold depending on the campaign's starting power level). Spend this on weapons, armor, and survival equipment from the Armory chapter. A few considerations:

  • Match your weapon to your proficiency. You choose a weapon proficiency when you take your first point in Combat (Melee) or Combat (Ranged). Using a weapon you are not proficient with imposes Disadvantage on both To-Hit and To-Wound rolls.
  • Heavier armor costs To-Cast rolls. If you are a spellcaster, read the Arcane Interference table before buying plate. Mithril is the exception — it carries no casting penalty.
  • Don't forget non-combat gear. Torches, rope, rations, and a bedroll are not glamorous, but they determine whether you survive the dungeon, not just the fight.

5 Define Your Hero

Mechanics are only half the character. Before you begin play, answer three questions about who your hero is:

  • What do they want? Every character needs a driving ambition — revenge, fortune, knowledge, redemption, survival. This is the engine of your role-play.
  • What do they fear? Fear is the world's most useful narrative tool. A character with no fear is a statue. Name one thing your hero cannot face without hesitation.
  • What do they carry from their past? One sentence. A debt, a loss, a vow, a secret. This is the thread the GM will pull.

Blood and Purpose rewards characters who are invested in the world. The dice decide outcomes — but the reasons you care about those outcomes are yours to write.

The Hero's Resources

Luck & Fortune DEfying Fate

Luck: Roll 1d10 at the start of each session. You may spend up to half (rounded up) of your total luck per single roll to achieve success or prevent a critical failure.

Fortune (Rerolls): For each point in the Fortune skill, you are granted one reroll per game session.

Action Points (AP) The Economy of Combat

Every hero starts with a base of 1 Action Point per turn. You can increase your total AP pool by investing in the Quickness skill or purchasing AP directly with XP.

  • Attacking: Swinging a 1H blade costs 1 Action, while firing a Heavy Crossbow requires 3 Actions.
  • Casting: The complexity of a spell dictates how many actions it takes to invoke.
  • Utility: Activating Focus, breaking free from vines, or drinking a potion typically costs 1 Action.
Holding & Carrying Actions — The Power of 3 AP Economy & Turn Patience

Holding an Action: A character may declare a Hold at any point during their turn, forgoing one or more remaining AP. The held action has a declared trigger — a specific event that must occur before the hold expires (e.g. "I hold my attack until an enemy enters my reach" or "I hold my shot until the mage begins casting"). If the trigger occurs, the character acts immediately — interrupting or responding to the triggering action before it resolves. If the trigger never occurs, the held AP is lost.

Carrying AP Over — The Power of 3: For every 3 points of base AP a character possesses, they may carry 1 unused AP into their next turn. This represents the physical and mental readiness built by restraint. Carried AP must be declared at the end of the character's turn and is lost if not used during the following turn — AP cannot accumulate across more than two consecutive turns.

AP Carry-Over Scale
Base AP Max Carry Typical Choice
3 AP 1 Use 2 now, carry 1 — or use all 3
4 AP 1 Use 3 now, carry 1
5 AP 1 Use 4 now, carry 1
6 AP 2 Use 4 now, carry 2 — or use 5, carry 1
9 AP 3 Legendary fighter — real tactical flexibility
Formula: floor(Base AP ÷ 3) = maximum AP that may be carried into the next turn.

Restrictions:

  • No type restriction. Carried AP may be used for any action — melee, ranged, defensive, utility.
  • No carry into channeling. Carried AP may not be combined with a Channeling or multi-turn casting action.
  • Two-turn limit. AP cannot accumulate across more than one carry. Use it on the following turn or it is gone.

Example: Baanon has 4 base AP. He uses 3 AP this turn to strike twice and reposition. He declares he is carrying 1 AP forward. On his next turn he begins with 5 effective AP — his standard 4 plus the 1 carried — and can spend them freely before the carry expires at the end of that turn.

Initiative & Surprise

Combat begins by rolling a single 1d10 vs the DM's 1d10. Add the Agility stat of the fastest player vs. the Agility stat of the fastest monster to the respective rolls.

Surprise: If attacking from a hidden position, the attacking party gains Advantage. An entirely undetected ambushing side gains Advantage (Roll 2d10, drop lowest) and a flat +2 to their result.

🏃 Phases & Movement

During your party's turn, you may move up to your race's distance. You can make a partial move, attack, then finish your move.

Sprint (1 AP): Move an additional 15 Ft. You may use this multiple times per turn if you have the Action Points available.

Terrain Penalties: Uneasy (-5 Ft), Rough (-10 Ft), Risky (-20 Ft).

👁️ Held Actions & Reactions

You may choose to save any number of your available Action Points (AP) to use during an enemy's turn.

  • Declaring a Stance (1 AP): To hold an action, you must spend 1 AP on your turn to enter a Reactive Stance. You must specify a trigger (e.g., "I will shoot if they cross the bridge") and the action being held.
  • The Interruption: When the trigger occurs, you immediately resolve your action. Your held action occurs before the enemy completes the action that triggered it.
  • The Reaction Penalty: All Held Actions suffer a -2 penalty to the To-Hit or To-Cast roll. This penalty is entirely ignored if the character has 6 or more points in the Discipline skill.
  • AP Carryover: You cannot "hoard" AP across rounds; any held AP not triggered by the start of your next turn is lost.

The Core Roll (To-Hit)

To score a hit against a target, or to successfully cast a spell, you roll 1d10. You must achieve a number equal to or higher than the target's Defense value or the spell's Target Number (TN). Apply your natural characteristics or skill bonuses to your result.

Advantage & Disadvantage

When the odds of combat shift, the dice reflect the advantage:

  • Advantage: Roll an additional 1d10. Select the highest score as your result.
  • Disadvantage: Roll an additional 1d10. You are forced to take the lesser score as your result.
Astounding Success (Exploding Dice) NATURAL 10 — ON A CONFIRMED HIT

If your To-Hit roll (1d10 + To-Hit bonus) meets or beats the target's Defense and the d10 result was a natural 10, the dice explode. Roll a second 1d10. No To-Hit bonus applies to this roll — only points in Precise Strike count.

  • If the raw second roll (+ any Precise Strike points) meets or beats Defense → you deal an extra 1d10 wounds on top of your original hit.
  • If it falls short of Defense → the chain ends. You still deal the original hit's wounds normally.

Chaining: If the second roll is itself a natural 10 and meets or beats Defense, you gain another extra 1d10 wounds and roll again. The chain continues as long as each natural 10 meets Defense. Precise Strike points apply to every roll in the chain.

The Miss (Natural 1) NATURAL 1

If your To-Hit d10 shows a natural 1, it is an automatic miss regardless of bonuses. No further penalties apply.

⚔️ Size-Up (1 AP)

If you are not currently engaged with multiple opponents, you may take a moment to find a gap in your target's guard.

  • Advantage: Gain Advantage on your next melee To-Hit roll.
  • Bonus Wounds: Add + Physique to the wounds caused on a successful strike.
Weapon Proficiencies

You must select a single weapon proficiency for your first point in the Combat (Melee) skill. Using a weapon you are not proficient with imposes Disadvantage on both To-Hit and To-Wound rolls.

  • Blunt: Club, Mace, Flail, Morning Star, Quarterstaff, Warhammers.
  • Edged: Axes, Swords, Claymores, Cutlasses, Falchions.
  • Piercing: Daggers, Lances, Pikes, Rapiers, Spears.
  • Polearm: Awl Pikes, Halberds, Staffs.
Weapon Stances & Choices
  • 1H-Weapon w/ Shield: Balanced defense and offense. No penalty To-Hit or To-Wound.
  • 2H-Weapon / Polearm: Devastating but unwieldy. Suffer Disadvantage To-Hit, but gain Advantage To-Wound.
  • Dual-Wield: Grants 1 additional attack per turn. Suffer Disadvantage To-Hit and gain no Advantage To-Wound. (Requires two Small/Medium weapons, or one Large and one Small/Medium).
  • Unarmed: Desperate brawling. Grants 1 additional attack. Suffer Disadvantage To-Wound against Leather armor or better.
  • Mounted: High ground advantage. Gain Advantage on To-Hit and To-Wound against smaller or equal-sized unmounted targets.
Tactical Situations

Backstab: Striking an unaware foe from the shadows deals 1d10 x 2 wounds. Requires a successful Stealth vs Awareness test.

Outnumbered: Swarming your prey. Outnumbering a target at least 2-to-1 in melee grants Advantage To-Hit.

Ranks: Weapons like spears allow To-Hit attempts from a 2nd-line rank with no penalty.

Disengage: Fleeing combat. Roll 1d10 + Agility Bonus + Skill Points to set the Defense Value (DV) the enemy must beat for their free strike against you.

🎯 Aim (1 AP)

You take a steadying breath and align your sights.

  • Advantage: Gain Advantage on your next ranged To-Hit roll.
  • Bonus Wounds: Add + Awareness to the wounds caused on a successful strike.
  • Vantage: Your maximum range is increased by 50% for this shot.

Note: Ranged weapons with high action costs (2+) represent reload time; a pre-loaded weapon requires only 1 Action for the first shot.

Weapon Proficiencies (Ranged)

You must select a proficiency for your first point in the Combat (Ranged) skill. Non-proficient weapons impose Disadvantage To-Hit.

  • Bow: Short Bow, Longbow, Recurve, Elven Bow.
  • Crossbow: Light, Standard, and Heavy Crossbows.
  • Throwing: Javelins, Knives, Axes, Spears. (Add Physique bonus to Wound rolls).
  • Black Powder: Pistols, Rifles, Blunderbuss.
Loading

Loading & Readying: Ranged weapons with high action costs (2+) represent the time needed to reload. A pre-loaded weapon requires only 1 Action for the first shot.

Line of Sight & Cover

Cover: Hiding behind trees or walls grants the target Disadvantage To-Hit against you. Targeting a specific individual in a group imposes a -1 penalty per obstructing target.

Firing into Melee: Shooting into engaged combat imposes Disadvantage To-Hit (unless the target is 2x the size of your ally). A miss does not hit your ally; the penalty simply represents you aiming safely.

Casting Mechanics

Into Melee: There is no penalty to cast when targeting units actively engaged in combat.

Resisting Magic: Targets may make Agility or Willpower tests to reduce wounds or evade effects. The DV they must beat is equal to your total To-Cast roll.

Miscast & Wrath: A natural 1 on a casting attempt is a Flub. Failing to spend Luck to prevent it results in a disastrous magical backfire on the Miscast/Wrath table.

🛡️ Casting & Armor — Arcane Interference

Heavy armor disrupts the concentration and attunement required for spellcasting. Each armor type carries a Tension Value (TN) that penalizes To-Cast rolls. This penalty is applied automatically by the character sheet.

  • Faith: Suffer half the TN value (rounded down). The divine connection is internal and spiritual — plate may weigh the body, but it cannot silence the soul.

✨ Counter-Casting (The Arcane Duel)

Masters of the weave can attempt to snuff out enemy magic as it is being formed.

  • Requirement: You must be in a Reactive Stance (1 AP) and hold enough Action Points to match the incoming spell’s AP cost.
  • Attacker Roll: 1d10 + To-Cast + Primary Skill Points.
  • Defender Roll: 1d10 + To-Cast + Primary Skill OR Resist Bonus.

Duel Outcomes:

  • Defender Wins: The spell fizzles instantly. All AP spent by the attacker is lost.
  • The Spell-Clash (Tie): The magic destabilizes and detonates at the midpoint. Both casters immediately take half of the intended spell's wounds (unmitigated).
  • Arcane Backlash (Nat 10 Win): If the Defender wins with a Natural 10, the attacker must pass a DV 10 Willpower test or suffer 1 💫 Stunned condition.

Example Arcane Duels

1. The Arcane Scholar (Wizard vs. Wizard):
A Rival Mage casts Fireball (Total Roll: 18). A Wizard uses their Wizardry Skill to dismantle it. The Wizard rolls a 9 + 4 (To-Cast) + 6 (Skill) = 19. The Wizard wins, and the Fireball dissipates into harmless smoke.

2. The Divine Refusal (Wizard vs. Priest):
A Necromancer casts Decompose (Total Roll: 14). A Priest doesn't know the spell, so they use their Resist Bonus to deny it. The Priest rolls a 5 + 3 (To-Cast) + 5 (Resist) = 13. The Priest fails, and the curse takes hold.

3. The Spell-Clash (The Tie):
A Shaman fires a Flame Burst (intended for 16 wounds). A Druid attempts to counter. Both casters calculate their totals and both hit exactly 15. The spells collide and explode; both the Shaman and the Druid immediately take 8 unmitigated wounds.

Arcane Mastery: Upon reaching 15 points in Wizardry, Shaman, or Faith, your connection to the weave deepens. You may now add your Spirit skill points as a flat bonus to all wounds caused or healed by your spells and blessings.

Status & Conditions

"The battlefield is cruel. Blood loss, terror, and broken bones will kill you just as fast as an enemy's blade."

↑ Return to Index

I. Physical Trauma

🩸 Bleeding (Stacking)

Lose 1d10 Wounds per stack at the start of your turn. Stacks are removed via a successful magical healing blessing, spell or potion.

🧪 Poison (Minor/Major)

Minor: Lose 1 Wound per turn.
Major: Lose Dd10 Wounds per turn. Pass a Willpower Test at the end of your turn to remove.

🦠 Disease

Lose 1d10 Wounds per turn. Additionally, suffer a -1 penalty to a random characteristic every 24 hours until cleansed by magic.

II. Tactical Penalties

💫 Stunned

You immediately lose 1 Action Point (AP). If you have no actions remaining this turn, you lose 1 AP from your next turn instead.

🧎 Prone

You have been knocked off your feet. You suffer Disadvantage on all To-Hit rolls, and enemies gain Advantage to hit you in melee. Standing up costs 1 Action.

🕸️ Pinned / Entangled / 🧊 Frozen

Pinned/Entangled: You cannot move. Attacks and casts are made at Disadvantage. Pass a Physique or Agility test to break free.

Frozen: Cannot move or take physical actions. You may only attempt Willpower-based casts or mental actions.

III. Mental & Sensory

😱 Fear (X)

Must pass a Willpower Test with a penalty equal to the Fear Rating (X). On a failure, you must spend all actions moving away from the source.

🌑 Blinded

All Melee and Ranged attacks are made at Disadvantage. Your Defense rating is reduced to your Armor value only.

🥀 Dismayed / 💖 Allure

Dismayed: You lose your Move action on your next turn and cannot perform Reaction actions (like Parry or Dodge).

Allure: Treat the source as a trusted ally. You will not attack them. Ends if attacked by the source.

IV. The Rule of Three

🌀 Overwhelmed

If you are ever afflicted by three or more different conditions at once, you are Overwhelmed. You cannot take any Reaction Actions (Dodge, Parry, Riposte) until at least one condition is cleared.

💤 Unconscious

Triggered at 0 Wounds. If healed to 1+ Wound, you move at ½ speed and require a Willpower test to perform an attack or complex action.

CHAPTER VIII

Growth & Advancement

"Experience is the best teacher, though its lessons are often paid for in blood."

↑ Return to Index
Earning Experience

In the Blood and Purpose, characters do not level up in the traditional sense. They earn Experience Points (XP) through trials, combat, and roleplay. The DM typically awards between 25 and 75 XP per session based on the magnitude of the party's achievements.

The Compounding Cost

Mastery is not easily attained and uses a Compounding XP Cost. The first rank is affordable, but subsequent ranks become increasingly expensive.

  • Rank 1: 15 XP
  • Rank 2: 30 XP
  • Rank 3: 45 XP
  • Rank 4: 60 XP

*Specializations or high-tier Masteries may have higher base costs but follow the same compounding logic.

Characteristic Advancement

Your six Core Characteristics are not static. For every 3 Skill Points allocated into skills belonging to a specific characteristic (Agility, Awareness, Charisma, Intellect, Physique, Willpower), that parent characteristic automatically increases by 1 point. Your stats represent the sum total of your specific training.

Skill Tiers & Classless Freedom

Requirements: Most skills are available to all, but Advanced Skills and Masteries require a foundation (e.g., you cannot master Wizardry without first understanding Knowledge).

Freedom: You are never locked into a path. A Wizard who finds a magical greatsword can choose to spend their next 100 XP on Melee proficiencies to become a battle-mage.

Weapon Special Rules

Heater & Heavy Shields
  • The Physique Gate: A massive slab of reinforced steel or ironwood forms an impenetrable wall, but its sheer bulk makes nimble footing nearly impossible. A character must have a minimum Physique of 4 to effectively wield a Heater or Heavy Shield.
  • Encumbrance Penalty: While equipped, the sheer weight of the shield imposes a -1 penalty to your Agility characteristic, affecting all associated tests and derived stats.
  • Over-Encumbered: If a character with a Physique of 3 or lower attempts to wield a Heavy or Heater Shield, they do not gain the Defense bonuses, and the Agility penalty is doubled to -2 until the shield is unequipped.
Ballistics & Penetration
  • Piercing Weapons: Black powder and Crossbows ignore the listed amount of Wound Mitigation. If the mitigation is lower than the piercing value, the extra damage is not added.
  • Minimum Damage: A successful combat strike always deals a minimum of 1 wound, even if the target's mitigation exceeds the damage roll.
  • Black Powder: Rifles ignore armor mitigation entirely unless the target is beyond long range. Blunderbuss sprays a 15 Ft AoE; targets must make a DV10 Physique test or suffer 1 Stunned condition.
  • Crossbows: Standard and Light Crossbows (2d10 wounds) negate armor mitigation at half-range. Heavy Crossbows (3d10 wounds) negate armor mitigation entirely (except magical bonuses) and cause 1 Bleeding condition on a To-Hit roll of 10.
  • Bows: Recurve bows reduce armor mitigation by half up to half-normal range.
  • Entangling: Lassos and Whips can entangle a target on a failed Agility test. The attacker chooses which limb is snared.

The Rule of Mastery

A caster’s power is strictly limited by their training. You may only attempt to cast a spell or blessing if its Target Number (TN) is equal to or less than your current points in the associated skill (e.g., 12 points in Faith allows you to cast blessings up to TN 12).

TN 0 — Fledgling Cantrips: Every casting tradition includes a small number of TN 0 cantrips — minor workings that require no formal training to attempt. Any character who has chosen a casting path (even with 0 XP spent) may attempt these freely. They cost 1 Action Point, deal no wounds, and cannot be overcast. They represent raw, untrained potential — the flicker before the flame.

DISCIPLINE VI

Runecraft

"To cast a spell is to whisper to the wind; to carve a rune is to command the stone to remember."

↑ Return to Index

Where a Wizard whispers to the weave and a Priest calls upon their god, a Runemaster commands — carving certainty into steel, stone, and bone. Runecraft is not spellcasting. It is a physical craft of binding magical effects permanently or temporarily into objects through precise runic inscription. The power is in the preparation.

Permanent Inscriptions

Permanent runes cannot be inscribed during an encounter. They require uninterrupted time, quality tools, and appropriate materials — they are crafted between adventures, in a workshop or camp, never under fire.

Each Permanent rune lists a TN and an AP cost. The TN is the difficulty of the inscription — rolled once when the work is completed. The AP cost represents how many full days of focused work the inscription demands (1 AP = 1 full day). A Master Rune costing 4 AP takes four full days of uninterrupted craft. Failing the TN roll means the work is lost and must begin again.

Once inscribed, a Permanent rune is always active — it requires no action to use. Its effect is constant, passive, and bound to the object until the rune is erased or the object is destroyed.

Temporary Inscriptions

Temporary runes can be inscribed during combat. They represent quick markings — scratched sigils, chalk lines, blood symbols — that a Runemaster carves onto a weapon, piece of armor, or talisman in the heat of battle. The AP cost shown is the number of Action Points spent on your turn to complete the inscription.

A Temporary rune is not active the moment it is carved. It sits dormant on the object until the bearer chooses to activate it. Activation is a free action — speaking or touching the rune-word costs 0 AP and can be done at any point on the bearer's turn, or as a reaction.

Once activated, Temporary runes either last for a set duration, last for 1 encounter, or are consumed on use — as specified in the rune's entry. A Temporary rune cannot be re-inscribed on the same object in the same encounter.

Important: A Runemaster may only inscribe a Temporary rune on an object they are holding or touching. They cannot inscribe at range.

Runecraft does not use the standard TN → AP casting table. It has its own scale reflecting the physical and mental demands of inscription work.

The Laws of Runecraft
  • The Rule of Three: No single object may bear more than 3 total runes — weapon, armor, or talisman alike. The material can only hold so much power before it begins to reject further inscriptions.
  • Mastery Limit: An object may only bear 1 Master Rune at a time. Master Runes are so demanding on the material that no second one can take hold alongside them.
  • No Duplication: The same rune cannot be carved twice on the same object. Effects do not stack. Multiplier runes such as Rune of Might and Master Rune of Skaif Black Hammer do not stack with each other or with any other To-Wound multiplier.
  • Material Quality: Permanent inscriptions require quality materials — crude iron, rotting leather, or a cheap bauble will not hold a Master Rune. The GM determines suitability. As a guide: a standard weapon or piece of armor qualifies, a masterwork object holds runes more reliably, a peasant's buckle does not.
  • Erasure: A Permanent rune can be removed by a Runemaster of equal or greater skill spending 1 full day of careful work, or it is destroyed with the object. Temporary runes fade naturally when their duration expires or are consumed on activation — they cannot be manually erased.

Runecraft & Other Disciplines

Runecraft is a craft, not a casting tradition. It does not channel the weave, invoke spirits, or call upon divine power — it binds magical law directly into matter through physical inscription. This means it can coexist with some disciplines and fundamentally cannot coexist with others.

Discipline Combined with Runecraft Reasoning
Faith ✔ Allowed Divine scripture carved into blessed steel. The holy warrior who inscribes sacred text into armour and weapons is one of the oldest warrior archetypes. Faith and Runecraft represent different expressions of the same devotion.
Shaman ✔ Allowed Ancestral runes and spirit-bound inscriptions are a coherent tradition. A Shaman who carves the names of ancestors into their weapons channels their strength in a natural extension of shamanic practice. Requires cultural or ancestral justification from the player.
Ki ✔ Allowed Internal mastery of the body's energy combined with external inscription of power into flesh and equipment. A Ki master with scripture bound into their skin matches outer discipline to inner. The two traditions do not conflict.
Wizardry ✘ Forbidden Wizardry commands the arcane weave through the mind — magic as a living system to be understood. Runecraft binds magic as physical law into matter. These are not different methods; they are opposed philosophies of what magic fundamentally is. A character may not invest in both. This restriction is absolute.
Druid ✘ Forbidden Druidic power is living, flowing, and seasonal — it cannot be frozen into permanent inscription without being destroyed in the process. The static binding of Runecraft is antithetical to the living current of druidic magic.

The forbidden combinations are absolute — no GM ruling may override them. They reflect how the magic systems fundamentally work, not an arbitrary restriction.
